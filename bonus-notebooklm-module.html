1 Why this matters

You're surrounded by documents — schemes of work, exam specs, textbook chapters, reports, research, policies. Reading, cross-referencing and summarising them eats hours. NotebookLM is Google's free research tool that turns a pile of your own sources into something you can question, summarise, and even turn into a revision podcast — and it shows you exactly where every answer came from.

It's the AI tool teachers tend to fall in love with, because it's grounded in real documents rather than making things up. This bonus module gives you the full tour.

2 The idea

An expert that has only read your sources

A normal chatbot (ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini) answers from everything it learned in training, plus the open web. NotebookLM is different: you give it a set of sources, and it answers only from those — citing the exact passage each time.

NotebookLMGrounded in the sources you upload. Every claim is footnoted to a source you can click and check. Far less likely to "hallucinate".
A normal chatbotDraws on general training and the web. Brilliant for drafting and ideas, but you can't always see where a fact came from.
The mental model: a normal chatbot is a clever generalist; NotebookLM is a research assistant who has read exactly the documents you handed over — and nothing else. That makes it trustworthy for working with specific materials.

4 Your turn — add some sources

Two ways to fill the Sources panel

A) Upload what you already have

  1. Click + Add source in the Sources panel.
  2. Choose a type: upload a PDF, pick a Google Doc or Slides, paste a website link or YouTube URL, or paste copied text.
  3. It's processed in seconds and appears in the list, ready to question. Add several — NotebookLM works across all your sources at once.

B) Let it find sources for you — "Discover"

No sources yet? NotebookLM can research the web for you.

  1. Click Discover (the magnifying-glass-with-sparkles, next to Add source).
  2. Describe your topic in plain English — e.g. "evidence-based strategies for improving literacy at Key Stage 3".
  3. It scans the web and returns up to ~10 relevant sources, each with a short summary of why it's relevant.
  4. Tick the ones you want and add them in one click. (Feeling adventurous? "I'm feeling curious" suggests a random topic.)
⚠️ Always sense-check discovered sources before trusting them — NotebookLM finds them on the open web, so check they're credible and current, exactly as you would with any research.
5 Use the chat panel

Ask, then verify with one click

Type a question in the centre panel. The answer comes back with little numbered citations — click one to jump to the exact passage in the source it came from. Some starters to try:

SummariseSummarise the key points across all my sources as ten concise bullet points a busy teacher could skim.
Cross-referenceWhere do these sources agree and where do they disagree? Quote the relevant lines and cite them.
Make it usableTurn the main ideas into a one-page explanation pitched at a non-specialist colleague, in British English.
The habit that matters: click the citations. Because answers are grounded and footnoted, you can verify in seconds — which is what makes NotebookLM trustworthy for real work.
6 The Studio — turn sources into outputs

One set of sources, many formats

The Studio panel (right) generates ready-made resources from your sources. Pick one, wait a moment, and it's made. The current line-up:

🎧Audio OverviewA podcast-style chat between two AI hosts — brilliant revision-on-the-commute. You can even join and ask questions live.
🎬Video OverviewNarrated visuals; choose explainer vs brief, language and a visual style.
🧠Mind MapA clickable diagram of how the ideas connect — great for the board.
📄ReportsAuto-written, cited documents: study guide, briefing doc, FAQ, timeline and more.
🃏FlashcardsKey-concept cards for retrieval practice.
❓QuizAuto-generated questions to test understanding.
🖼️Slide DeckA presentation drafted straight from your sources.
📊InfographicA visual one-pager of the key points.
🗂️Data TablePulls structured facts into a sortable table.

You can run several at once — listen to an Audio Overview while a Mind Map or Study Guide generates. Everything is built from (and cited to) your sources.

7 🛡️ Data-safety checkpoint

Mind what you upload

NotebookLM is a brilliant tool — but it's a Google product, and our school's approved tool for real pupil/staff data is Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat (the green shield). So:

  • Great to upload: your own non-personal materials — schemes of work, your lesson resources, exam specs, published research, public documents, textbook chapters you're licensed to use.
  • Never upload: anything with identifiable pupils or staff in it — class lists, mark books, reports, SEND/EHCP paperwork, safeguarding notes, or photos of pupils.
  • NotebookLM doesn't use your uploads to train its models — but that doesn't replace school approval or data minimisation. Personal Google accounts have no data agreement with the school.
✗ Don't upload"Year 11 mock results & intervention notes (with names).xlsx"
✓ Fine to uploadThe exam board spec + your revision booklet, then build a quiz and audio overview from them.

Rule of thumb: if it names a real child or colleague, it doesn't go in. Keep it to documents, not data about people.
